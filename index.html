Chain of custody

What actually happened,
with dates.

No conspiracy, no spin, no takes. Just the public record of an object that shaped an election cycle and then a court case, from the reporting of the outlets that chased it.

April 2019

A laptop is left at a repair shop in Wilmington, Delaware. The shop owner says the customer identified himself as Hunter Biden. It is never collected.

December 2019

The FBI seizes the machine under a grand jury subpoena. Investigators later conclude it was genuinely his and showed no sign of tampering.

October 14, 2020

The New York Post publishes emails from the drive, three weeks before the election. Twitter and Facebook restrict the story's distribution the same day.

October 19, 2020

Fifty-one former intelligence officials sign a letter calling the material consistent with "a Russian information operation." The Director of National Intelligence states publicly that it is not part of a Russian disinformation campaign. Both statements stand in the record.

March 2022

The Washington Post and the New York Times authenticate a portion of the emails. Five hundred days after the story that nobody was allowed to share.

November 2022

A CBS News forensic analysis finds the data consistent with ordinary everyday use, with no evidence of modification or fabrication.

June 2024

Federal prosecutors enter the laptop into evidence at trial. It becomes an exhibit. It never becomes something you can look at.

September 9

It becomes a contract address on Base. Two of them, on the same day. Which is, at this point, the most accessible form the thing has ever taken. Check which one you're buying.

Sources: New York Post (Oct 14–15, 2020) · Washington Post and New York Times forensic analyses (Mar 2022) · CBS News (Nov 2022) · court filings, United States v. Biden (Jun 2024).

Questions

Straight answers.

Is this connected to Hunter Biden's token?

No. A separate LAPTOP token launches on Base on September 9, 2026 with him on the founding team — the same chain and the same day as this one. This project is independent, deployed from a different wallet, with no affiliation to him or to that team. Two contracts will share this ticker: the address is the only thing that tells them apart, so check it before every transaction.

What happens when I contribute?

You choose an amount of ETH or USDC on Base and confirm a direct payment to the displayed organizer wallet. No tokens are sent to you by that payment. The organizer plans a manual distribution after launch and decides token amounts later, without a fixed rate or guaranteed allocation.

What's the utility?

None. No staking, no revenue share, no roadmap of products, no treasury doing anything clever. It's a memecoin: a ticker, a story, and whatever price a market agrees on.

Who holds the supply?

The token has not been created yet. Its supply and holder distribution are not known. After launch, check the published contract and transfers on Basescan.

Can I lose money?

All of it, fast, and most people who buy memecoins do. $TRUMP peaked near $15 billion in market cap and now trades around $600 million — down about 97% — and that one had a sitting president attached. Contribute only an amount you can afford to lose. No token allocation or return is guaranteed.
